# Nitrogen Management

## Why timing beats rate

Nitrogen applied before the crop can take it up is nitrogen at risk. Nitrate is
mobile in soil water and leaches below the root zone with rain or excess
irrigation; urea broadcast on a warm, moist surface loses nitrogen to ammonia
volatilisation within days if it is not incorporated.

Matching supply to demand therefore matters more than the total rate on the plan.
The same annual quantity produces very different results depending on when it
arrives.

## Split application

Splitting the dose across growth stages is the standard response. In winter wheat
a typical pattern places a modest amount at sowing to establish the crop, the
largest share at tillering when demand accelerates and yield potential is being
set, and a smaller late application at stem elongation or booting, which
influences grain protein more than grain number.

In maize under drip irrigation, fertigation extends this logic further: many small
applications across the season track uptake closely and keep the soil nitrate pool
small at any moment, which is precisely what limits leaching loss.

## Choosing the source

Urea at 46-0-0 is the cheapest nitrogen per unit but the most vulnerable to
volatilisation, and it needs incorporation or irrigation immediately after
spreading.

Ammonium sulfate at 21-0-0 carries sulfur and acidifies the fertiliser band, which
is a genuine advantage on high-pH calcareous soils where micronutrient
availability is the limiting factor.

Diammonium phosphate at 18-46-0 is primarily a phosphorus source with a useful
starter nitrogen component, normally placed at sowing.

## The legume credit

A preceding legume leaves residue with a low carbon-to-nitrogen ratio that
mineralises rapidly, supplying a meaningful nitrogen credit to the following crop.
A chickpea crop ahead of wheat or maize typically justifies reducing the planned
nitrogen rate rather than applying the standard amount and accepting the surplus,
which would otherwise be lost rather than used.
