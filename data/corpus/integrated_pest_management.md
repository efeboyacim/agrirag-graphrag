# Integrated Pest Management

## What IPM is

Integrated pest management is a decision framework, not a product category. It
treats chemical control as one tool among several and applies it only when
cheaper, lower-risk measures have been exhausted or have demonstrably failed.
The sequence is preventive first, responsive second: choose a resistant
cultivar, rotate the crop, time the sowing away from peak flights, conserve
natural enemies, and only then reach for a spray.

The defining feature is that an intervention must be justified by an
observation. A calendar spray programme is by definition not IPM, even when the
products used are selective or organically approved.

## The four pillars

Prevention removes the conditions a pest needs. Rotation starves soilborne
pathogens of a host, resistant cultivars block infection outright, and sanitation
removes the residue that carries inoculum between seasons.

Monitoring establishes what is actually present. Scouting on a fixed pattern,
pheromone traps for adult flights, and sticky traps for small flying insects all
convert a vague worry into a number.

Thresholds convert that number into a decision. The economic threshold is the
pest density at which the expected crop loss exceeds the cost of controlling it.
Below the threshold, spraying loses money twice: once on the product and again on
the natural enemies it kills.

Intervention, when justified, is chosen for selectivity. A product that spares
predators and parasitoids leaves the field better defended against the next
generation than a broad-spectrum alternative with the same immediate knockdown.

## Why it holds up economically

Farmers who adopt threshold-based spraying typically cut applications rather than
yield. The saving is not only the unbought product; it is also the avoided
resistance selection, the shorter pre-harvest interval constraints at harvest
time, and the reduced risk of a residue exceedance that makes a consignment
unsellable.

The main cost of IPM is attention. It requires someone in the field on a regular
schedule who knows what they are looking at, which is a labour cost that a
calendar programme does not carry.
