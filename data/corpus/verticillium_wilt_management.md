# Verticillium Wilt Management

## The pathogen

Verticillium dahliae is a soilborne fungus that enters through the roots and
colonises the vascular system, restricting water movement. Symptoms are one-sided
wilting, leaf yellowing and premature defoliation, usually appearing as the crop's
water demand peaks during flowering.

Its defining feature is persistence. The fungus forms microsclerotia, resting
structures that survive in soil for ten years or more without a host. This single
property determines everything about how it must be managed.

## Why there is no cure

No effective curative treatment exists for an infected plant, because the pathogen
is inside the vascular tissue where a foliar application cannot reach it.
Management is therefore entirely about reducing inoculum and avoiding exposure.

Rotation works, but slowly. Non-host crops starve the microsclerotia of a feeding
opportunity and the population declines through natural mortality alone, which
means a two-year break barely moves the needle. A cereal such as wheat between
cotton crops is a genuine non-host and reduces build-up over successive cycles,
but this is suppression measured in years.

## What else helps

Soil solarization inactivates microsclerotia in the upper profile and is one of
the few interventions with a measurable effect within a single season. Its limits
apply: only the heated layer is treated, and deeper inoculum recolonises over
time.

Trichoderma harzianum applied to the root zone suppresses infection through
competition, parasitism and induced resistance. It is a moderate-efficacy tool
rather than a solution, and it establishes far better in a solarized soil where
competing organisms have been reduced.

Avoiding the problem is cheaper than managing it: planting an olive orchard on
land previously cropped to cotton exposes a long-lived perennial to an inoculum
load that was built by a different crop and will outlast the decision by decades.

## Host range

Cotton and olive are both susceptible, which matters in regions where the two
occur together. Rotating cotton with a cereal rather than another susceptible host
is the basic preventive discipline.
