# Maximum Residue Levels

## Definition

A maximum residue level is the highest concentration of a pesticide residue
legally permitted in or on a food or feed commodity. In the European Union these
are set under Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, with EFSA providing the scientific
assessment.

An MRL is a trade and good-practice standard rather than a toxicological safety
limit. It reflects the residue expected when the product is used correctly at the
authorised rate and interval. A consignment above the MRL may be refused entry
even where the residue poses no acute health concern.

## Why exporters care disproportionately

For a crop sold domestically, national rules govern. For a crop destined for the
EU market, the EU MRL applies regardless of where it was grown. This makes
residue compliance a commercial issue concentrated in export-facing production:
Aegean olives and table grapes, Black Sea hazelnuts, and Cukurova cotton and
citrus all face it directly, while a crop consumed domestically does not.

The practical effect is that the same active substance may be perfectly legal to
apply and still commercially unusable, because the resulting residue would close
the export market.

## The default limit

Where no specific MRL has been established for an active substance and crop
combination, a default limit at the limit of determination applies, typically
0.01 mg/kg. This is effectively a detection-level threshold and means an
unapproved substance is treated as not permitted rather than unregulated.

When an approval is withdrawn, the MRL is usually lowered to this default. That
is what happened to chlorpyrifos under Regulation (EU) 2020/18: approval was not
renewed, authorisations were revoked, and the residue limit dropped to the limit
of determination, closing the market to any consignment carrying detectable
residue.

## Managing the risk

Residue compliance rests on three unglamorous habits: keeping application records
that show rate and date, respecting pre-harvest intervals exactly, and testing
consignments before shipment where the buyer requires it. Most exceedances trace
back to a missed interval rather than an overdose.
