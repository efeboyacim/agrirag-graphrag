# Olive Fruit Fly Management

## The pest

Bactrocera oleae is the key pest of olive across the Mediterranean. Females lay
single eggs into developing fruit from late summer into autumn, and the larva
feeds inside the drupe.

The damage is qualitative as much as quantitative. Infested fruit drops early, and
oil extracted from infested fruit carries elevated free acidity, which downgrades
it out of the extra virgin category. For table olives, any visible larval entry
makes the fruit unsellable.

## Monitoring

Yellow sticky traps with a pheromone or food attractant track adult activity, and
fruit sampling for oviposition punctures establishes actual infestation. Decisions
rest on the percentage of fruit with viable punctures, with a low threshold for
table olives and a somewhat higher one for oil.

## Mass trapping

The olive fruit fly is unusually well suited to mass trapping: it has essentially a
single host, immigration is limited where whole blocks are covered, and removing
females before oviposition directly prevents damage. At 20 to 100 traps per
hectare, maintained on schedule and deployed across the whole block rather than a
few rows, mass trapping can hold infestation below threshold for a season without
insecticide.

The failure mode is partial coverage. A trapped block surrounded by untreated
groves receives continuous immigration and the traps cannot keep up.

## Bait applications

Spinosad applied as a protein bait spray is the standard supplementary treatment.
Because it is applied as a bait to a portion of the canopy rather than as a cover
spray, the quantity used is small and non-target exposure is limited. It is
permitted in organic production and carries a seven-day pre-harvest interval.

Deltamethrin is also used but is broad-spectrum, kills the parasitoids and
predators that suppress the fly, and carries a fourteen-day interval. In
export-oriented Aegean production the residue considerations under EU maximum
residue levels reinforce the preference for the bait approach.
