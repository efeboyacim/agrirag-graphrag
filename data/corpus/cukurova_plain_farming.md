# Farming on the Cukurova Plain

## The setting

The Cukurova is the alluvial delta plain formed by the Seyhan and Ceyhan rivers on
the eastern Mediterranean coast. The climate is Mediterranean - mild wet winters,
hot dry summers - with roughly 650 mm of annual rainfall and a mean temperature
near 19 C.

Soils are deep and heavy, dominated by alluvial clays with high water-holding
capacity and, in places, drainage and sodicity problems.

## Intensity

The long frost-free season supports double cropping: a winter crop harvested in
May or June followed by a summer crop in the same year. Cotton and maize dominate
the summer, wheat the winter. Yields are high by national standards - maize
commonly around 9.5 t/ha, seed cotton near 4.8 t/ha.

That intensity is also the region's pest problem. Continuous cropping with short
gaps and overlapping host availability sustains pest populations that a single
annual crop cycle would interrupt.

## Soil management

The heavy clays are the defining management challenge. They crack when dry and
seal when wet, and the window for tillage at the right moisture content is narrow.
Where sodicity has developed, gypsum is the appropriate amendment: it supplies
calcium to displace exchangeable sodium, after which leaching removes the
displaced sodium. Leaching without gypsum on a sodic soil worsens dispersion.

Subsoiling to break plough pans restores downward water movement where compaction
has developed under repeated heavy machinery traffic.

## Pest pressure

Cotton bollworm and whitefly are the major cotton problems through the summer, and
verticillium wilt is an established soilborne constraint that builds under close
cotton rotations. Fall armyworm and corn borer affect maize. Yellow rust can
appear on winter wheat.

Because much of the region's output is export-facing, EU maximum residue levels
are a live commercial constraint, and the withdrawal of chlorpyrifos under
Regulation (EU) 2020/18 removed a product that had been widely used here.
