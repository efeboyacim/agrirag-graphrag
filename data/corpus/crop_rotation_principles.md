# Crop Rotation Principles

## The mechanism

Rotation works because most serious soilborne pathogens and many weeds are host
specific. Remove the host for long enough and the population declines through
natural mortality with no reproduction to offset it. The required break length is
a property of the pathogen: some decline in a single season, while others persist
as resting structures for years.

A rotation is therefore designed backwards from the most persistent problem in
the field, not from the most profitable crop in the plan.

## Designing a sequence

The basic rule is to alternate botanical families, because related crops share
pathogens. Beyond that, alternate rooting depths so the profile is explored
evenly, and alternate sowing dates so that weeds adapted to one window do not
build up unchecked.

Legumes deserve a place in almost every sequence. They fix atmospheric nitrogen
and leave a residue with a low carbon-to-nitrogen ratio that mineralises quickly,
so the following cereal needs less fertiliser nitrogen. In Central Anatolia a
wheat-chickpea sequence does both jobs at once: it breaks the cereal foliar
disease cycle and supplies a nitrogen credit to the next wheat crop.

## Persistence varies enormously

Verticillium dahliae forms microsclerotia that survive in soil for a decade or
more, so a two-year break barely dents the population and rotation must be
treated as a slow suppression strategy rather than a cure. Cercospora beticola
survives mainly on infected beet residue, so a three-year break from sugar beet
is usually sufficient. Ascochyta rabiei overwinters on chickpea stubble, and a
three-year break combined with clean seed is the standard recommendation.

## What rotation cannot do

Rotation is ineffective against highly mobile pests. Airborne rust spores travel
hundreds of kilometres, so no rotation on a single farm affects yellow rust
pressure. Migratory insects such as fall armyworm arrive from outside the
rotation entirely. For these, resistant cultivars and monitoring do the work that
rotation does for soilborne problems.
