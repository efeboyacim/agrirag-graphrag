# Pre-Harvest Intervals

## What the PHI is

The pre-harvest interval is the minimum number of days that must pass between the
last application of a plant protection product and harvest. It is set so that
residues decline below the legal maximum residue level by the time the crop is
picked, and it appears on every product label.

The PHI is a legal requirement, not a recommendation. Harvesting inside it is a
compliance breach whether or not the residue actually exceeds the limit.

## Why it constrains late-season decisions

Late infestations are where the PHI bites. A pest that appears three weeks before
harvest eliminates every product whose interval is longer than three weeks,
regardless of how effective those products are. The available choice narrows to
short-interval materials, which are frequently the biological ones.

Spinosad with a three to seven day interval and Bacillus thuringiensis with
effectively none remain usable when a pyrethroid at fourteen days or a triazole at
thirty-five days no longer does. This is a common reason for a late-season switch
to biologicals that has nothing to do with organic certification.

## Planning around it

The practical consequence is that late-season product selection should be
considered at the start of the season, not when the pest appears. Mapping the
expected harvest date against the intervals of the products in the plan shows
which materials will still be legal in the final weeks, and that mapping should
influence which products are bought.

Stacking applications does not change the arithmetic: the interval runs from the
last application of each product, so a late corrective spray of a long-interval
material can delay harvest past the optimum date.
