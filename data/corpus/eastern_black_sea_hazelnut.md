# Eastern Black Sea Hazelnut

## The setting

The Eastern Black Sea coast around Ordu and Giresun has a humid subtropical
climate with annual rainfall above 1200 mm distributed through the year, mild
winters and a mean temperature near 14.5 C. High humidity is the year-round norm.

Soils are acidic forest soils with pH between 4.5 and 6.0 and organic matter
often above four percent - the highest in any of the regions covered here, the
product of high biomass production combined with slow decomposition under cool,
wet, acidic conditions.

## Why hazelnut fits

Hazelnut is well matched to this acidity, thriving in a pH range that would cause
aluminium toxicity problems for most field crops. Its water requirement of around
900 mm is comfortably met by rainfall without irrigation. The steep terrain that
makes mechanised field cropping impractical is workable for a perennial shrub
crop.

The result is a near-monoculture across the coastal hills, with the crop grown in
multi-stemmed bushes on slopes too steep for alternatives.

## Constraints

Constant humidity elevates fungal disease pressure generally and makes drying
after harvest a critical operation; inadequate drying leads to mould development
and aflatoxin risk, which is the principal quality and market-access hazard for
the crop.

The hazelnut weevil is the main insect pest, with adults emerging in spring and
females laying into developing nuts. Control depends on timing an application to
the adult emergence period, since once the larva is inside the nut it is
unreachable. Deltamethrin and spinosad are both used, with spinosad's shorter
pre-harvest interval and organic compatibility favouring it where certification
or residue limits matter.

Because the great majority of the crop is exported, EU maximum residue levels are
the operative standard, and the region hosts a significant certified organic
sector.
