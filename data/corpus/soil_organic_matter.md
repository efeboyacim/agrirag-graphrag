# Soil Organic Matter

## Why it matters disproportionately

Organic matter is a small fraction of soil mass - commonly one to four percent in
the systems described here - but it disproportionately controls how a soil
behaves. It binds mineral particles into stable aggregates, holds water and
cations, buffers pH change, and feeds the biology that cycles nutrients.

Two soils of identical texture but different organic matter content behave like
different soils in the field.

## Regional contrasts

The calcareous soils of the Konya basin sit near one percent organic matter. Low
rainfall limits biomass production, hot summers accelerate decomposition, and the
long history of cereal monoculture with residue removal has drawn the reserve
down. Aggregates are weak, the surface crusts after rain, and infiltration
suffers.

The acidic forest soils of the Eastern Black Sea reach four percent or more.
Abundant rainfall drives high biomass production while cooler, wetter conditions
and acidity slow decomposition. The resulting soils have excellent structure and
water-holding capacity.

## Building it

Reserves rise slowly and fall quickly. Returning crop residue rather than removing
or burning it is the largest single lever on most farms. Reducing tillage slows
the oxidation that follows each cultivation. Cover crops add carbon during what
would otherwise be a bare fallow. Compost and farmyard manure add it directly,
along with nutrients.

None of these produce a visible change in a single season. A measurable rise of
half a percentage point commonly takes five to ten years of consistent management,
and one season of intensive tillage with residue removal can undo several years of
accumulation.

## The realistic goal in dry regions

Under 320 mm of annual rainfall there is a ceiling on how much biomass can be
produced and therefore on how much carbon can be returned. In the Konya basin the
practical objective is to arrest decline and make incremental gains, not to reach
the levels a humid region sustains without effort.
