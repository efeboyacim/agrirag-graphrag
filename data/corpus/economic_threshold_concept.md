# Economic Thresholds

## Definition

The economic injury level is the pest density at which the value of the damage
equals the cost of preventing it. The economic threshold, sometimes called the
action threshold, is set slightly below that point, because a control measure
takes time to work and the population keeps growing while it does.

Setting the threshold below the injury level is what buys the operator time. How
far below depends on how fast the population doubles and how long the chosen
control takes to act.

## What moves a threshold

Thresholds are not fixed constants. A high crop price raises the value of the
protected yield and therefore lowers the threshold: intervention becomes worth it
at a lower pest density. A cheap, effective product does the same. Conversely, a
low-value crop or an expensive treatment pushes the threshold up.

Growth stage matters as much as density. The same number of larvae per square
metre is trivial during vegetative growth and serious during grain filling,
because the plant can compensate for early leaf loss but cannot replace lost
grain.

## Practical use

In cereals, sunn pest thresholds are expressed as overwintered adults per square
metre at emergence and as nymphs per square metre during grain filling, with the
grain-filling threshold being far lower because damage at that stage degrades
baking quality rather than simply reducing tonnage.

In cotton, bollworm decisions rest on egg and small-larva counts on a fixed
number of plants per field, checked twice weekly through the flowering period.
Counting eggs rather than damage is what makes the decision preventive instead of
reactive.
