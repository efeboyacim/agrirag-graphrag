# Sunn Pest Management

## The pest

Eurygaster integriceps is the most economically significant insect pest of wheat
and barley in Turkey, Iran and the surrounding region. Adults overwinter under
leaf litter in mountain habitats, then migrate into cereal fields in spring as
temperatures rise.

Damage occurs in two distinct phases. Overwintered adults feeding on stems early
in the season kill the growing point and produce whiteheads, causing direct yield
loss. Nymphs feeding on developing grain during grain filling inject enzymes that
degrade gluten.

## Why the quality damage dominates

The second phase is the expensive one. Grain damaged during filling looks nearly
normal but its gluten has been degraded, and dough made from it does not develop
properly. Milling grades reject consignments above a low threshold of damaged
kernels - often around two percent - so a crop with full tonnage can still be
downgraded from bread wheat to feed.

This is why the grain-filling threshold is far lower than the early-season one and
why the economics of control differ from most insect pests: the loss is to price,
not to yield.

## Control

Thresholds are expressed as overwintered adults per square metre at crop emergence
and as nymphs per square metre during grain filling. Scouting on a fixed pattern
through the spring is the basis of the decision.

Where treatment is justified, synthetic pyrethroids such as deltamethrin and
lambda-cyhalothrin are the standard materials, both with a fourteen-day
pre-harvest interval that must be checked against the expected harvest date.

Breaking a continuous cereal sequence with a non-host crop reduces local carryover
in stubble, which is part of the case for chickpea in the Konya and GAP rotations.
Threshold-based scouting rather than calendar spraying is the single largest lever
on the number of applications, and it conserves the natural enemies - egg
parasitoids in particular - that suppress the next generation.
