# Yellow Rust Management

## The disease

Puccinia striiformis causes yellow or stripe rust, producing yellow pustules in
stripes along wheat leaves. It favours cool, moist conditions and can develop
explosively in a wet spring, with the potential to remove a large fraction of
yield when it establishes early and the season stays favourable.

Spores travel on wind over long distances, which has two important consequences:
an epidemic can arrive in a region where the pathogen did not overwinter, and no
rotation practised on a single farm affects the risk.

## Resistant cultivars come first

Genetic resistance is the foundation of management. Cultivars carrying effective
Yr resistance genes block or slow pustule development, and in a resistant cultivar
a season's fungicide programme may be unnecessary entirely.

The complication is that the pathogen evolves. New races overcome specific
resistance genes, sometimes across a wide area within a few seasons, and a
cultivar that needed no fungicide one year may need one the next. Race surveys and
regional advisory information are therefore part of cultivar selection rather than
an academic exercise.

Partial or adult-plant resistance, which slows rather than blocks infection, tends
to be more durable than single-gene resistance precisely because it exerts weaker
selection on the pathogen.

## Fungicides

Where a susceptible cultivar is grown or an epidemic develops, triazoles such as
tebuconazole and strobilurins such as azoxystrobin are both effective, and both
carry a thirty-five-day pre-harvest interval that constrains late applications.

Timing matters more than product choice: an application at the first appearance of
pustules protects the upper leaves that contribute most to grain filling, while a
spray applied after the canopy is already heavily infected recovers little.

Alternating modes of action across applications is the standard resistance
precaution, and it matters most for the strobilurin group, where resistance is
governed by a single mutation and can appear abruptly.
