# Fall Armyworm Management

## The pest

Spodoptera frugiperda is a migratory lepidopteran pest of maize that spread
rapidly across Africa, Asia and the Mediterranean basin after 2016. Larvae feed in
the whorl during vegetative growth, producing characteristic ragged holes and
frass, and later attack ears directly.

It has no diapause and breeds continuously wherever temperatures permit,
producing overlapping generations through the summer. In the Cukurova and GAP
regions the hot season supports several generations; in Konya it appears in
irrigated maize during summer.

## Why timing dominates

Control depends almost entirely on reaching small larvae. Early instars feeding on
the whorl surface are exposed and susceptible; once larvae move deep into the
whorl or into the ear they are physically protected, and the same product that
would have worked a week earlier fails.

Scouting for egg masses and early whorl damage, rather than waiting for visible
ragged leaves, is what makes intervention effective. Pheromone traps time the
flight and start the clock.

## Products

Bacillus thuringiensis subsp. kurstaki is effective against small larvae, has
essentially no pre-harvest interval, and spares natural enemies. Spinosad is
effective and permitted in organic production, with a three-day interval. Both
require thorough coverage directed into the whorl, where the larvae actually are.

Chlorpyrifos was formerly used against this pest but its approval was not renewed
under Regulation (EU) 2020/18, authorisations were revoked, and the residue limit
was reduced to the limit of determination. It is no longer an available option in
production facing EU markets, and it is restricted under Turkish plant protection
rules as well.

## Resistance

Fall armyworm has developed resistance to multiple insecticide groups across its
range, including to spinosad in some populations. Rotating modes of action and
relying on threshold-based rather than calendar applications are the practical
countermeasures.
